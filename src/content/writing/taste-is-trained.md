---
title: Taste Is Trained
description: Taste is not subjective. It comes from seeing enough good and bad work and paying attention to the details.
date: 2026-11-12
---

People like to say taste is subjective. In this era of AI, I hear it more than ever. If anyone can generate anything, the thinking goes, then who is to say what is good?

I disagree. I believe taste comes from seeing enough good work and enough bad work, and paying attention to the details.

## What people have said

The argument is old. David Hume wrote that taste is something you practise. Some people judge better than others because they have seen more, compared more, and put their own prejudices aside.<sup><a href="#fn1" id="fnref1">1</a></sup>

Kant saw it differently. A judgement of taste starts as a feeling, not a rule. Yet when we call something beautiful, we speak as if everyone should agree.<sup><a href="#fn2" id="fnref2">2</a></sup>

Bourdieu was more critical. He found that what people like follows their class and education, so taste can mark where someone belongs.<sup><a href="#fn3" id="fnref3">3</a></sup>

All three are partly right. Taste is personal, it can be learned, and the people around you shape it.<sup><a href="#fn4" id="fnref4">4</a></sup>

## How to build it

There is no shortcut. You look at enough good work that you can feel the difference between good and bad.

## My own practice

I started being intentional about my taste this year, though I already had a strong background in design. I got into graphic design at 15, and I was making poster designs back then. Whenever I walked down the road, I noticed flyers and posters that looked great, and I tried to use what I learned from them in my own work.

That habit never stopped. As a builder today, I keep improving my taste by exposing myself to more work, both good and bad. What makes your work stand out is the care you put into the details.

## In short

When someone is said to have high taste, it means they have looked at a lot of good work. It is trained, not given. And when anyone can make anything, choosing well is what is left.
